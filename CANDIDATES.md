# Local judging — Describe Your Ex in One Word

Judged against the published judge brief, criteria in order:
1. The word really is a one-word verdict on an ex (a word that does not describe an ex loses to any that does).
2. Comic punch: lands as a joke at a glance — twist, unexpected angle, precise absurdity, not a plain insult.
3. Surprise: not the obvious choice, startles while staying instantly clear.
4. Craft: exactly one word, spelled correctly, no punctuation tricks, meaning arrives without a caption.
5. Recognition: anyone who has had an ex feels the truth in it.

Field note (API payload, 25 Nov 2026 ~07:11 UTC, 362 submissions): the field is
saturated with Unsubscribed (32+), Unsubscribe (20+), Recalled (19+), plus
Uninstalled (6), Refund (5), Expired (4), Unmatched (3), Redacted (2),
Nonrefundable (2), Deprecated (2), Repossessed (2). Byte-identical entries share
one rate and the earlier receipt places above its copies, so a saturated word is
a bad pick even before the joke is weighed. All candidates below are original
and were checked absent (or near-absent) from the field.

## PRIMARY — Discontinued

1. Verdict: yes — the ex as a product line that has been discontinued. The whole
   model pulled from the catalogue, no restock coming. Unmistakably a verdict on
   a former partner.
2. Punch: strong — the joke lands at a glance: a person treated as a SKU whose
   production run ended. Precise absurdity, not an insult.
3. Surprise: good — same product family as the field's "Recalled", but a
   different, fresher verdict (killed the line vs. sent one unit back), and
   0 occurrences in the field.
4. Craft: one clean word, correctly spelled, no tricks.
5. Recognition: high — everyone has seen a "discontinued" label and felt the
   finality of it.

## Runners-up

### Clearance
Ex marked down on the clearance rack to get rid of them. Good punch and
surprise, one clean word, high recognition. Loses to Discontinued on criterion 1:
"clearance" is about price/status, while "discontinued" is a verdict on the
thing itself — the model is gone for good.

### Ick
"The ick" — sudden repulsion. Very punchy, very surprising, one clean word,
huge recognition with anyone who dates. Loses to Discontinued on criterion 1:
it names a feeling about the ex more than a verdict on the ex, so it falls to
any word that more directly describes the ex.

### Unfollowed
You unfollowed your ex. Strong on criterion 1, one clean word, recognition
near-universal. Loses on punch and surprise: it is the obvious social-media
move, a plain report rather than a twist.

### Ghosted
Directly a verdict on an ex, one clean word, recognition very high. Loses on
punch and surprise: it is the most obvious word in the space — a description,
not a joke.

### Muted
You muted your ex like a chat. Cute, recognisable, one clean word. Loses on
punch: muting is a quiet administrative act, the joke is thin compared with
killing off a whole product line.

## Rejected

- **Terminated** — HR/contract verdict, funny at first glance, but reads as a
  job ending rather than a relationship ending; weaker on criterion 1.
- **Spam** — "my ex is spam" is readable, but spam is something you delete
  unseen; the verdict is on the message, not the person. Weak on criterion 1.
- **Undeliverable** — "return to sender" is a nice image, but the word is long
  and the joke needs a beat to arrive; loses on punch and craft.
- **Deactivated** — account deactivated. Same family as Uninstalled (6 in the
  field); no surprise, thin punch.
- **Corrupted** — file corrupted. A verdict on a file, not clearly on a person;
  weak on criterion 1.
- **Unlisted** — property/listing verdict. Reads as "not for sale", which is
  almost a compliment to an ex; weak punch.

## Verdict

Submit **Discontinued**. It is the only candidate that is top-tier on all five
criteria in order, and it is byte-distinct from everything in the field.
